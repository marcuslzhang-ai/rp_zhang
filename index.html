A Study of the Neural Network Reduction Through the Partial Enforcement of Tared Weights

Marcus Zhang


Background

Neural networks, versatile AI models inspired by the human brain, excel in numerous tasks such as image recognition and natural language processing, but fall short in efficiency and effectiveness due to an issue of overfitting, which occurs mainly due to the excessive parameter count in the network, causing models to overadapt during training and thus underperform when seeing new data. Many solutions have been proposed to address this issue, including those of Dropout (2012, shown in the left image) and Dropconnect (2013), which utilize the partial pruning of weights to reduce the overall neural network complexity.

Rationale

Despite the large amount of effective solutions proposed to reduce levels of overfitting in neural networks (pruning and non-pruning), there remains a lack of comprehensive research and comparisons between methods on a general scale of tasks. Furthermore, a lack of hybrid methods which may strategically incorporate the strengths of Dropout and Dropconnect layers into one model has been observed and studied, which may outperform conventional full Dropout or Dropconnect models.

Methodology / Procedures

Using the MNIST dataset (Displayed left) as a universal benchmark image-classification task, a series of neural networks implemented with non-drop methods, Dropout methods, and Dropconnect methods, were trained and tested with equivalent parameters to ensure fair comparisons between methods. Each model type was trained 10 times, and eventual epoch data was gathered and statistical analysis was performed to find the most effective method of overfitting reduction.
